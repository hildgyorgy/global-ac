# Season dial

A curved brass fader follows the front half of the levitation disk. DECEMBER
is on the left and JUNE on the right; the central engraved Sun and the small
Earth on the grip make this a schematic half-orbit instrument.

Drag the grip or tap the rail. Keyboard users can tab to the equivalent native
range: arrows change the setting, Page Up/Down take larger steps, and Home/End
select December/June. Dial gestures suspend camera navigation until release.
The opaque globe occludes picks. Projection and its transitions disable the dial;
returning to globe view restores the selected setting. RESET preserves it.

The whole globe, axis, guides and atmosphere turn together around the vertical
through the globe centre. Daily rotation continues independently. Obliquity
stays at the configured 23.4 degrees. The fixed directional key is horizontal at
globe-centre height, with the same azimuth as the reading lamp; the lamp's local
point light remains at the actual bulb. The key gives solar declination
−23.4 degrees in December, zero midway, and +23.4 degrees in June.

This is a relative-coordinate tabletop demonstration: changing the instrument
heading represents moving to another orbital position relative to the Sun.
Earth's axis does not actually turn through half a revolution in space over
six months. The central Sun is an engraving, not another light source, and the
large levitating globe does not translate around it. The fader follows orbital
angle rather than a calendar with exact dates. The meridional diagram retains
its canonical readable orientation while the globe unfolds.

Season changes illumination and orientation only. Circulation, temperature
colours, jet positions and prescribed regional highs remain the same idealized
model; there is no seasonal atmospheric response yet.
