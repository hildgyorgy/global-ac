# Atmospheric model and display conventions

This is an educational analytical circulation field, not a numerical weather
simulation. It prescribes motion rather than solving pressure, momentum,
energy, moisture or mass conservation. Apparent particle density represents
sampling for legibility, not air density. Real circulation is variable and
asymmetric; the three-cell diagram is an idealized mean.

## Meridional circulation and temperature

`src/atmosphere/HadleyModel.ts` defines alternating cells in each hemisphere:
Hadley from the equator to 30°, Ferrel from 30° to 60°, and polar from 60°
to the pole. Lower branches flow equatorward in Hadley/polar cells and
poleward in Ferrel cells, reversing aloft. Air rises at the equator and
polar front, and sinks at the subtropics and poles. These are imposed
kinematics: in particular, the Ferrel cell is not dynamically generated by
weather-system heat and momentum transport in this prototype.

Temperature is a prescribed function of latitude and normalized altitude.
The altitude gradient makes ascending parcels cooler and descending parcels
warmer, but no parcel energy equation or moist/dry adiabatic process is solved.
The CPU reference returns kelvin. The TSL adapter normalizes that temperature
using the model's minimum and range before the common blue–violet–red ramp.
Temperature does not depend on the library lamp or current date.

## Geometry and sampling

`src/config.ts` sets the particle budgets, time scaling and exaggerated shell
thickness. `src/atmosphere/geometry.ts` defines surface clearance, shell thickness,
particle radius and the radial velocity metric. `GeometryNode.ts` is its TSL
adapter. The shell is thicker at the equator and thinner at the poles. These
are visual conventions, not a claim that Coriolis directly determines
atmospheric thickness. Altitude is normalized within this shell, not kilometres.
Vertical motion is visually exaggerated relative to horizontal circulation.

Ordinary particles retain their configured cell allocation on respawn.
Latitude sampling favours cell boundaries without inserting visible gaps.
Supplementary jet markers favour jet cores. Neither sampling bias represents
mass accumulation. Particle positions, velocities, lifetime, reseeding and
trail history stay on the GPU; CPU updates are limited to global controls.

## Coriolis response

`CoriolisModel.ts` prescribes an eastward velocity response `du/dt = f v − drag u`,
where `f = 2 Ω sin(latitude)` and northward `v` comes from the maintained
meridional circulation. Equatorward flow deflects west; poleward flow east.
This is not a coupled solver for both horizontal momentum components.

Angular velocity and display time are exaggerated. A symmetric visual taper
reduces the zonal acceleration and longitude-rate limit near the poles while
retaining the physical sine factor. Latitude metrics are regularized to avoid
longitude singularities. Positive damping removes residual drift when disabled.

## Jet streams

`JetStreamsModel.ts` defines mirrored subtropical and polar-front bands near
30° and 60°. The subtropical core sits higher than the polar-front core.
These are broad idealized positions; real jets move and meander. See
[NOAA's jet-stream overview](https://www.noaa.gov/jetstream/global/jet-stream).

Each core is a latitude/altitude Gaussian with a lower-air exclusion gate.
At full weight the target total speed is twice the background speed, with
an explicit reference-speed floor near stagnation. Only the eastward component
is changed; northward and vertical circulation continue carrying particles
through the bands. Jets are imposed after the Coriolis longitude cap, independently
of its switch. They do not feed back on circulation or temperature and are not
derived from pressure gradients or thermal-wind balance.

Extra jet markers seed near each core, then follow the same field. Markers
that drift away fade and reseed, so they are not physically trapped. Their
colours, size and trail selection follow the ordinary particles. Core-marker
density is a display aid, not additional atmospheric mass.

## Projection and lighting

The meridional view projects a finite longitude wedge onto its middle plane,
preserving latitude and radius. It is not a physical slice. East–west motion
continues in 3D but is collapsed in the projection. Its enhanced brightness,
near-surface haze and horse-latitude belts are graphical annotations, not
computed temperature, pressure or density fields.

The reading lamp provides artistic lighting through a local point light and
an aligned shadow-casting directional light, plus faint ambient fill. It is
not a live astronomical Sun model or physically computed global illumination.
